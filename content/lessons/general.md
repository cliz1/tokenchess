In Xiangqi, the equivalent to the King is traditionally carved with Shuài 帥 or Jiàng 將, both meaning ”General.” 
 In Token, the King can optionally be replaced by this piece.

---

@note Corner and checkmate the opposing general.

The General moves one square orthogonally, never diagonally. 
 As a result, the King dominates it one-on-one.

```challenge
fen: "8/1g6/8/3K4/8/8/8/8 w - - 0 1"
line: d5c6 b7a7 c6b6 a7a8 b6b7
```

---

The General's trick is his ability to *capture* like a rook. This is an adaptation of Xiangqi's [Flying General rule](https://www.xiangqi.com/history).

```challenge
fen: "8/1k6/p7/1p6/3P2P1/8/PP6/G6q w - - 0 1"
line: a1h1
```

---

@note In this diagram, black is forced into a corner, after which the stalemate wins for white.

In the event of stalemate, if the side delivering the stalemate has a General, the result is a win for that side rather than a draw. Interestingly, this allows a General to win by itself against another.

```challenge
fen: "8/1g6/8/2G5/8/8/8/8 w - - 0 1"

```
